# Prompt card (print one per team)

## How it works

Your game starts as an empty world you can walk around in. You plan on the whiteboard, then tell the AI your
plan (type it, or send a photo of the whiteboard). It builds something, you play it, and it offers new
directions. Answer with a number ("2") or your own idea.

**Stage 1: your world, character and story.** Where does your game happen? Who is your main character, and
what is their name? What has happened in this world (three short sentences)? What should the player feel?

**Stage 2: your goal and rules.** What is the player trying to do? What can they control? What makes it
tricky? How do they win or finish?

*Mechanics* means: the actions and rules that make your game work.

The AI shows where you are: **World** → **Player** → **Goal** → **Mechanics** → **Details**. You decide when to
move on: pick the 🚪 option, or just start talking about the next thing.

Tell the AI the **big picture**, not just the next object: "we want it to feel like a cozy decorating game" or
"like a racing game, but with dragons". It will suggest ways to get there.

## Your link 🔗

Every answer from the AI ends with a link to your game. Give it to your host for the game list: other teams
and the big screen play your game through it, and they see every change you make. The link works while your
laptop is on and open, so **don't close the lid**. If the AI says "New link!", tell your host. Don't put real
names or photos in the game: anyone with the link can see it.

## Working together

One of you uses the laptop. The other draws, plans, asks questions and tries the game. **Swap every 20 to 30
minutes.** Choose the next change together: ask for one small change, try it, then decide what comes next.

## The recipe for a good prompt

1. **WHAT** I want: "Skeletons climb out of the ground and chase us."
2. **HOW it should feel**: "Slow and creepy at first, faster every wave."
3. **WHAT it looks like**: point to your sketch, a model from the catalog, or a screenshot.
4. **NUMBERS** I want to control: "Put their speed and health on sliders."
5. **HOW I'll test it**: "We should see 3 skeletons and lose health when they touch us."

Bad: "make it better". Good: all five, in your own words. Long is fine!

## Bug card (copy it into the chat)

> **I did** ... **I expected** ... **Instead** ...

## Things you can type to the AI

| Type | It does |
|---|---|
| `$idea` | plan the next stage or a big feature together (it asks you questions first) |
| `$explain` | explain what just changed, plus a Code Detective challenge |
| `$bug` | fix something broken |
| "go back to the last save point" | undo what just went wrong (the AI saves every time something works) |
| "give us the link" | your game's link, checked |

## The three prizes

- **Best world and story:** an interesting place, a memorable character, and a story that fits.
- **Most innovative game mechanics:** a clever or surprising way to play.
- **Audience preference:** the game everybody liked most.

## Rules

- Original only: no characters or names from other games, shows or movies.
- No real names (first names in credits are OK), no school, no photos of faces.
- Three tries and it still doesn't work? Ask the AI to go back to your save point, or ask a mentor.
